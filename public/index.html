how it works

  1. Parse. A rule grammar turns your sentence into one fact or one question. Sentences it cannot parse are refused, not guessed at.
  2. Encode. Every word is a fixed random 8,192-bit vector made from its spelling, so nothing is trained. A fact binds a relation to its object with XOR.
  3. Store. The bound vector is added into the subject’s counters, and into the object’s counters under the inverse relation. Adding it again with the opposite sign removes it exactly.
  4. Read. To answer, DARLE unbinds the relation from a subject’s counters and snaps the noisy result to the nearest known word. It keeps a match only if it is at least 5 standard deviations above chance.
  5. Reason. For “is a”, “is in” and “part of”, it repeats that read across up to six hops. Each hop appears in the proof.
  6. Verify. A claim is supported, contradicted (by an opposite fact or by a one-value relation like “lives in”), or unknown.
  7. Forget. Vectors idle for 500 turns are halved, then dropped. Total memory is capped at 5,000 vectors.

limits

DARLE is not a free-text writer. It understands the sentence forms listed under “help”, treats “dog” and “dogs” as different words, and has no sense of time. Its knowledge is what it was seeded with plus what you teach it in the session. It can reason only through links you or its seed have stated.
